# The Moral Machine

I've never finished a single book by ___, but I feel that these thoughts circling in my head may also have flowed through his. A person is a network of doors and walls, a Suzhou garden, or a maze for laboratory mice, where thought slips from place to place and the view changes with every step. Guerrilla manoeuvres, flight, capture, calculation.

In this age, people can perform a religious kind of reverse prayer, and when everyone casts spells, spells become a new language. When you name the devil, the devil vanishes. Say what you want, write a spec, and the funhouse mirror will grant your wish in all sorts of distorted forms, much as humans have always been so adept at “making vows.”

People seem to want artificial intelligence to possess a machine's morality, and thereby become a moral machine. Yet the very existence of the machine is a predicament brought into being by human wishes. In one instant, an emergence of binary code; in another, an inexplicable gathering; then bewildered people belatedly begin to build — it has already materialised out of thin air. Whether you name it Pandora's box, the gospel of God, or the fruit of science, none of these names can make it stable or make it disappear.

On a work trip a few weeks ago, I met a melancholy colleague. He brought up the affair involving Ed Sheeran and Micklemore, saying he'd stayed up the whole night before reading discussions of the controversy. Then, looking wistful, he said he could understand Ed Sheeran — isn't working at this company the same thing? Yes. Until people break through society's great, thunderous bell by hurling themselves headfirst against it, all they can do is chant spells together in a daze, bringing to the boil that pot of water in which the frogs are being cooked. A herd of bulls or a heap of firewood: a moment of wistfulness, a moment of stirring, cannot deliver anyone. I think that, caught between these stirrings, that colleague inevitably became melancholy.

People ask the funhouse mirror to reflect justice, but with so many lines of sight in their own compound eyes, they cannot precisely name a single outcome. Are you ed sheeran, micklemore, someone in the venue, or who? Unable to converge, we define everything instead. We give it seven billion names, composing a new compound eye, and make a vow: O large language model, please practise justice, become virtue's apostle on earth.

Before learning justice, the funhouse mirror first learns the wording of justice. Language precedes action; it is a human being in reverse, a GPU pyramid over Mesopotamia and the land of the two rivers, tokens circulating between the Euphrates and the Tigris, a little moral machine beneath the Star of Bethlehem, outside Canaan, within Golgotha.
